## 1. The question

**One sentence.** IHDM's prior and level spacing were tuned on natural photographs. Can brain MRI
benefit from setting them to its own properties: its spectrum, and the anatomy that registered
subjects share?

**The argument, in four steps:**

1. **The defaults encode natural-image assumptions.** IHDM starts its chain from a training image
   blurred to $\sigma_{B,\max} = W/2$ and spaces its 200 levels logarithmically (Rissanen et al.,
   ICLR 2023). Log spacing puts the same number of levels in every octave, which matches data whose
   between-image variance is the same in every octave: the $1/f^2$ signature of natural images
   (Field 1987). W/2 erases almost all of a seed image, which suits photographs: no two scenes share
   a layout.
2. **Brain MRI departs from both assumptions.**
   - *Spectrum.* Its variance sits in mid frequencies: 29.8% in 8–16 c/img against 4.1% in the
     coarsest octave on IXI. On LSUN Churches the coarsest octave holds 23.8%.
   - *Anatomy.* After rigid registration every subject shares the same coarse layout: the mean
     training slice is itself a brain, while the mean church is a sky-over-ground gradient.
3. **The proposal: match the two settings to MRI.**
   - A smaller terminal blur (W/8) lets the prior hand over the coarse anatomy, which is cheap to
     inherit because subjects share it.
   - A level spacing fitted to the IXI spectrum spends steps where MRI's variance is.
4. **The test.** A 2 × 2 factorial on IXI (terminal blur × spacing; arms A0 = the paper's defaults,
   A1, A2, A3 = both changes), with OASIS-1 as an independent cohort.

**What changed from the proposal, and how the paper says it.**

- **The proposal.** It posed a cross-domain interaction (MRI against photographs). Its photograph
  arms (LSUN Churches, Bedrooms) were meant as the domain the defaults were tuned for.
- **What happened.** At our budget those models do not reach usable sample quality, so the
  interaction cannot be read.
- **How the paper presents them.** As expected at this scale, *not* as a failure of the method.
- **What the paper must still state.** In one place (Methodology or Results, two or three
  sentences) and one appendix table:
  - the natural-image runs were trained;
  - why they are out of reach at this budget;
  - that the cross-domain comparison of the proposal is therefore not reported.

  Leaving them out without a word would be selective reporting. The proposal is a graded document
  that names them.

## 2. Why the natural-image runs are out of reach at this budget (the text the paper can use)

**Three reasons, each with its evidence and label.**

| reason | evidence | label |
|---|---|---|
| **Compute.** The paper's single LSUN Churches model saw 32M images (1M iterations × batch 32, App. B.2). Ours see 0.96M (3%), with a U-Net 2.6× smaller (61 M against 160 M parameters). | `results_discussion.md` §3 | measured / cited |
| **Brain MRI is a narrower distribution.** Registered slices share their anatomy, and the local intrinsic dimension of the training set is lower: 17–22 for IXI and OASIS-1 against 28–31 for Churches and Bedrooms. Data of lower intrinsic dimension are easier to learn (Pope et al., ICLR 2021, arXiv:2104.08894). | the scratch measurement below; **to be regenerated by ticket T8.0** before it enters the paper | measured (pending T8.0) + cited |
| **Our framing of the photographs** made them harder: 192² native centre crops are zoomed parts of scenes. Whole-scene 128² framing (the paper's) raised Inception precision from 0.009 to 0.196 in a one-seed check. | `photo_diagnostic/README.md` | measured (post hoc, one seed) |

**The intrinsic-dimension measurement, done 2026-10-06 as a scratch check by main** (not
repository code). Method:

- the 3,200 training images, each image's DC removed, then centred;
- the Levina–Bickel maximum-likelihood estimator, with MacKay–Ghahramani averaging of the inverse
  (Levina & Bickel, NeurIPS 2004).

| dataset | MLE-ID, k = 10, N = 3,200 | k = 20, N = 3,200 | k = 10, N = 320 (random) | PCA participation ratio | components for 90% of variance |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| IXI | 17.2 | 17.9 | 16.5 | 42.5 | 635 |
| OASIS-1 | 22.0 | 21.7 | 19.1 | 60.7 | 815 |
| LSUN Churches | 30.1 | 27.6 | 21.1 | 25.0 | 859 |
| LSUN Bedrooms | 30.8 | 27.1 | 19.5 | 29.3 | 479 |

**Read it honestly.**

- **The local, nonlinear dimension is lower for MRI.**
  - Its estimate is already stable at N = 320: IXI +4% and OASIS-1 +15% from N = 320 to 3,200.
  - The photograph estimates keep rising with N: +43% and +58%. This is the known signature of the
    estimator under-sampling a higher-dimensional set.
  - With one slice per subject at N = 320, all four datasets read 16–23, so the separation needs the
    full training sets.
- **The linear measures do not support "lower-dimensional".** The PCA participation ratio is
  *higher* for MRI (42–61 against 25–29). The photographs put their variance in a few coarse modes
  (sky and ground gradients), and the number of components for 90% of the variance is mixed.
- **So the paper should say** "a lower *intrinsic* (local) dimension", cite the estimator, give
  both N, and not claim a lower linear dimension.

## 3. Objectives as they will appear in the paper (they replace the proposal's O1–O3)

- **O1 (prior).** Does a terminal blur matched to MRI's shared anatomy (W/8 instead of W/2)
  improve sample fidelity on brain MRI, and what does it cost in memorisation and diversity?
- **O2 (spacing).** Does a level spacing fitted to the IXI spectrum improve fidelity over log
  spacing, on its own and on top of W/8?
- **O3 (generalisation).**
  - Do the effects hold on a second cohort (OASIS-1: different scanner, older subjects)?
  - Does the gain hold for subjects the model never saw (held-out seeds)?

## 4. The claims ledger: what the paper may state, and on what evidence

Every sentence of Results and Conclusions must map to a row.

| # | claim | evidence | label |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | The natural-image defaults are not the best choice for brain MRI. The MRI-matched configuration (A3) lowers LSD by 68%, KID by 63% and FID by 51% on IXI, raises precision +0.09, recall +0.13 and coverage +0.31 (3 seeds, all agree), and keeps its signs on OASIS-1 (LSD −52%, KID −49%; 2 seeds) | tables 2a, 6; `results_discussion.md` §4, §7 | measured, pre-registered endpoints (KID, LSD) |
| C2 | Nearly all of the gain is the terminal blur: A1 carries 90% of the LSD gain and 101% of the KID gain. The spacing alone gives −11% (LSD, KID; 2 seeds agree), about a sixth, and nothing detectable on top of W/8 | table 4; §6 | measured |
| C3 | The mechanism. Models with the default prior under-disperse: their samples are too alike, with 41–50% of the real variance at 1–4 c/img (head outline, ventricles). The W/8 prior hands over the subject's coarse anatomy and removes that deficit | fig. 2; X1/X3; fig. 4 | measured + interpretation (labelled) |
| C4 | The cost is copying. 72% of training-seeded samples have their own seed subject as nearest training image (6% at W/2), and within-seed diversity falls 23% | tables 1b, 3 | measured |
| C5 | The per-sample realism gain holds on unseen subjects (held-out seeds: precision gain +0.098 against +0.102). Most of the KID gain (86%) does not, so the population-level gain relies on seeding from many real anatomies | `heldout_fidelity/README.md` | measured, post hoc, rule pre-registered |
| C6 | Every model under-disperses, and LSD mostly measures that | exploratory X1–X3 | measured, exploratory |
| C7 | Natural-image reference runs are out of reach at this budget, as expected (§2 above) | §3; T8.0 | measured + cited |
| C8 | When IHDM, rather than diffusion or flow matching, is the right tool: anatomy-preserving variation of a given scan; an explicit spectral "how much to keep" knob. When it is not: unconditional quality; data meant to be shared | the harness H-P4 | argued: one Discussion paragraph, no experiment |

**What the paper must not claim:**

- **"The schedule must depend on the data's spectrum in general."** Only one domain was tested at
  a usable quality.
- **"W/8 is better," without the copying cost** and the held-out-seed nuance (C4, C5).
- **"The spacing matters"** beyond the small W/2 effect (C2).
- **Significance.** p_min is 0.1; "CI excludes 0" means only that all seeds agree in sign.
- **A replication of Rissanen et al.** State the opposite: `results_discussion.md` §3 and §10
  item 8.

## 5. Title options (the proposal's convention: short, no question, no colon)

1. **Matching the Prior and Level Spacing of Inverse Heat Dissipation Models to Brain MRI** (recommended)
2. Anatomy-Aware Priors for Inverse Heat Dissipation Models on Brain MRI
3. Spectrum-Matched Blur Schedules for Inverse Heat Dissipation Models on Brain MRI (the proposal's)

Option 1 names both changes and the domain, and does not promise a spectral "law". Option 2
foregrounds the finding: the prior is what matters. Option 3 over-weights the spacing, whose effect
was small.
