## Why

The paper's framing (`00-framing.md` §2) explains why the natural-image runs are out of reach at
our budget. Part of that explanation is that registered brain MRI has a **lower intrinsic
(local) dimension** than photographs. Main measured this in a scratch script on 2026-10-06. This
ticket turns it into a committed, tested, reproducible result.

**The scratch numbers**, to be reproduced within ±1:

| dataset | MLE-ID, k = 10, N = 3,200 | k = 20, N = 3,200 | PCA participation ratio | components for 90% of variance |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| IXI | 17.2 | 17.9 | 42.5 | 635 |
| OASIS-1 | 22.0 | 21.7 | 60.7 | 815 |
| Churches | 30.1 | 27.6 | 25.0 | 859 |
| Bedrooms | 30.8 | 27.1 | 29.3 | 479 |

## Method (frozen)

- **The data.** The `train` split of `ixi`, `oasis1`, `lsun_church` and `lsun_bedroom` (3,200
  images each, from `$IHDM_DATA_ROOT/<ds>/images.npy`). Scale to [0, 1], remove each image's mean
  (DC), then centre across images.
- **The MLE intrinsic dimension** of Levina & Bickel (NeurIPS 2004), with the MacKay–Ghahramani
  average of the *inverse* local estimates:

  $$\hat m = \Big[\tfrac1N\sum_i \tfrac{1}{k-1}\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\log\tfrac{T_k(x_i)}{T_j(x_i)}\Big]^{-1},$$

  where $T_j(x)$ is the distance to the $j$-th nearest neighbour, the point itself excluded.
  - Use Euclidean distances from the Gram matrix, in float64.
  - k ∈ {5, 10, 20}.
- **Sample-size dependence.**
  - N ∈ {320, 1,000, 3,200}.
  - For N < 3,200, average 10 random subsets (rng 2026) and report the mean ± sd.
  - MRI also gets "one slice per subject": slice 5 of the 320 training subjects (N = 320).
- **Linear measures, for contrast:**
  - the PCA participation ratio $(\sum\lambda)^2/\sum\lambda^2$;
  - the number of components for 50%, 90% and 95% of the variance (eigenvalues of the Gram
    matrix).

## The reading (state it as written; it is not a hypothesis test)

- **The local (MLE) dimension** at N = 3,200 is lower for MRI than for photographs. The MRI
  estimates also change less from N = 320 to N = 3,200 than the photograph estimates do; this is the
  estimator's known signature of a higher-dimensional set.
- **The linear measures do not show MRI as lower-dimensional.** The participation ratio is higher
  for MRI.
- **The wording the paper can use:** "a lower intrinsic (local) dimension". Do not write "a
  lower-dimensional data set".

## Tests

1. **Synthetic data of known dimension.** Points on a d-dimensional random linear subspace of
   $\mathbb R^{1000}$ (d ∈ {5, 10}, N = 2,000, small isotropic noise). The estimate must fall
   within 20% of d.
2. **The Gram-based distances** equal the direct pairwise distances (`np.testing.assert_allclose`).
3. **The participation ratio** of a diagonal covariance with known eigenvalues equals its closed
   form.
4. **The CLI** exits 2 on a missing dataset folder.
