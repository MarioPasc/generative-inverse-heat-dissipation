**Template geometry, measured (not assumed)** — `nibabel` on
`tpl-MNI152NLin2009cAsym_res-01_T1w.nii.gz`: shape `(193, 229, 193)`, zooms `(1, 1, 1)`,
affine `diag(1, 1, 1)` with offset `(-96, -132, -78)`, axcodes `('R', 'A', 'S')`. The brain mask
shares the affine and is binary `{0, 1}`. SimpleITK reads the same file with
`direction = diag(-1, -1, 1)` (the RAS→LPS flip) and `origin = (96, 132, -78)`, so **the SimpleITK
voxel index order equals the nibabel one** and no permutation is needed anywhere between the two
libraries. Therefore: MNI origin voxel `(i0, j0) = (96, 132)`; the 192 window is `i ∈ [0, 192)`,
`j ∈ [36, 228)`; `z_mm(k) = k - 78`, i.e. the ten planes are
`[-23, -15, -7, 1, 9, 18, 26, 34, 42, 50]` mm. All of this is *derived from the affine and
asserted* in `mri.template_geometry`, never hard-coded.

**Decisions taken during the work** (each with the alternative rejected and why):

- **Optimiser family: `SetOptimizerAsRegularStepGradientDescent`, not LBFGSB.** Mattes MI with
  random 20 % sampling is a *stochastic* objective; LBFGSB builds a quasi-Newton curvature
  estimate from successive gradients and its line search assumes a smooth deterministic
  function, so sampling noise degrades it, and it is not the recipe SimpleITK's own rigid
  examples use. Regular-step gradient descent only needs the sign of the directional change (it
  multiplies the step by `relaxationFactor` when the gradient reverses), which is robust to that
  noise; combined with `SetOptimizerScalesFromPhysicalShift` the step is a physical displacement,
  so `learningRate = 1.0` means one millimetre. The metric sampling **seed is fixed (2026)**, so
  the objective is deterministic and the pipeline is reproducible; rejected the default
  wall-clock seed, which would make two runs of the same subject differ.
- **`gradientMagnitudeTolerance = 1e-8`, i.e. the gradient convergence test is switched off**;
  SimpleITK's default `1e-4` rejected. MEASURED on six IXI subjects at `minStep = 1e-4`: the
  default fires while the metric is still improving and leaves a visibly worse optimum
  (IXI014 -0.11919 against -0.13516, IXI015 -0.11037 against -0.13747, IXI012 -0.20088 against
  -0.20417); `1e-3` is worse still (IXI013 stops after *zero* iterations at -0.09236 against
  -0.14912). The step-length criterion is the only reliable stopping rule here.
- **10 mm dilation of the brain mask by exact Euclidean distance map**
  (`SignedMaurerDistanceMap(..., useImageSpacing=True) <= 10 mm`) rather than
  `BinaryDilate` with a ball structuring element of radius 10 voxels — the distance-map threshold
  is the exact 10 mm morphological dilation for any spacing (it does not depend on the template
  being 1 mm isotropic), and it is O(N) instead of O(N·r³). `BinaryDilate` rejected because a
  discrete ball of radius 10 is both slower and only an approximation of a 10 mm ball.
- **OASIS-1 axis map ported verbatim** from `analysis/unprocessed_loaders.py::load_volume_ras`:
  `volume = np.transpose(np.squeeze(raw), (2, 0, 1))[::-1]` with
  `zooms = (zooms_raw[2], zooms_raw[0], zooms_raw[1])`, giving index order `(i→R, j→A, k→S)` and
  spacing `(1.25, 1.0, 1.0)` mm. nibabel's SPM2-Analyze affine (`diag(-1, 1, 1.25)`) is
  **ignored**: Analyze 7.5 carries no usable orientation and that affine is nibabel's guess. The
  ported map was established empirically (mid-plane contact sheets plus translation-aligned
  cross-correlation against the FOMO60K copy of the same scan, 12/12 subjects, mean normalised
  cross-correlation margin +0.048). **This ticket re-verified the left–right polarity
  independently and it holds.** Twelve OASIS-1 subjects were registered twice with identical
  settings, once with the ported map and once with the left–right axis reversed, and scored
  against the *asymmetric* MNI152NLin2009cAsym template: the ported map gives the better
  converged mutual information on **12 of 12 subjects** (mean -0.12700 against -0.12379, mean
  margin +0.00321; exact sign test against a fair coin, two-sided *p* = 2 · 2⁻¹² ≈ 4.9 × 10⁻⁴).
  This is independent of the original evidence, which compared against the FOMO60K copies
  rather than the atlas. The `flip_lr` switch in `load_sitk` exists only for this check and is
  never used by the pipeline; `tests/preprocess/test_mri_raw.py` pins both the map and the fact
  that the switch is an exact mirror.
- **IXI loaded straight through `sitk.ReadImage`** — no nibabel `as_closest_canonical`
  reorientation. The IXI NIfTI affine is valid and slightly oblique (measured:
  `axcodes ('P', 'S', 'R')`, direction cosines off-axis by ~0.12); SimpleITK keeps the exact
  direction cosines in physical space, so the registration works in true physical coordinates and
  the single resampling absorbs the obliquity. `as_closest_canonical` rejected because it *rounds*
  the direction to the nearest axis-aligned one, which is an uncorrected rotation of up to ~7° for
  these volumes and would be baked into the data.
- **The registered volume is cached, not the slices.** Sidecar JSON carries the metric, the
  iteration count, the stop condition and the transform parameters, so the quality gate can be
  recomputed without re-registering and a `--limit` dry run warms the cache for the full run.
- **MAD in the failure gate is the raw median absolute deviation** (`median(|x − median|)`), not the
  consistency-scaled `1.4826 · MAD`. The ticket writes "median + 3 × MAD" without a constant, and
  the raw form is the stricter of the two (for a Gaussian it cuts at ≈2.02 σ, ≈2.2 % of subjects,
  which is what "< 5 % expected" in the acceptance list describes; the scaled form would cut at
  3 σ ≈ 0.13 % and would effectively disable the gate). Both thresholds and both failure counts are
  reported in `registration_report.md` so the choice is auditable.
- **The iteration-cap failure criterion was measured to be non-diagnostic and, on the
  orchestrator's instruction, is now recorded rather than applied.** The ticket froze both
  "max 300 iterations per level" and "FAIL ... or stop by max iterations". MEASURED on IXI with
  every other frozen value unchanged: roughly half the subjects exhaust the cap at the finest
  level *with the metric already converged* (IXI002 reaches -0.15442 both at the cap with
  `minStep = 1e-4` and after a normal stop with `minStep = 1e-2`), so applying the criterion
  would have excluded about 290 of 581 IXI subjects and made the 400-subject target
  unreachable. Three escapes were ruled out by measurement rather than argument:
  `relaxationFactor` 0.5/0.4/0.3/0.2 (IXI002 hits the cap at all four, so the number of step
  reversals is not the constraint), `learningRate` 1.0/0.5 (same), and SimpleITK's default
  `gradientMagnitudeTolerance = 1e-4` (stops early at a visibly worse optimum). The fourth,
  `minStep = 1e-2`, converges on real heads but leaves **6.03 degrees** on the synthetic
  phantom of acceptance criterion 5, against 0.176 degrees at `1e-4`, so it was rejected: the
  fine `minStep` is the margin that protects against the step collapsing before the optimum on
  a noisy Mattes gradient. `main` was messaged with this evidence and amended the contract on
  2026-09-22: **FAIL is the metric rule alone, plus any subject flagged by eye on the
  registration sheet**; the cap is recorded per subject, with its cohort rate, in
  `registration_report.md` and `meta.json.parameters.quality_gate`. `--exclude` implements the
  by-eye half and every exclusion is listed in `meta.json`.

- **`counts` carries two padding numbers, not one.** `padding_fraction` is the contract's
  "mean fraction of exactly-zero pixels" (`03-data-format.md` §5), which on these cohorts is
  dominated not by padding but by the acquisition's background noise being quantised down to
  zero: with the foreground p99 around 590 raw units, a background voxel of 1-3 units maps to
  `round(255 * 2 / 590) = 1` or `0`, so roughly half the background rounds to zero. Measured on
  nine IXI subjects: 20.3 % of pixels exactly zero, against the ~6.9 % of *columns* that
  `learning/03` §19 attributes to the 180 mm left-right field of view. `fov_padding_fraction` is
  therefore added: the mean fraction of the stored window falling outside the acquisition,
  obtained by resampling a constant image (never the data, so the one-interpolation rule holds)
  with nearest-neighbour interpolation. T1.3 needs the two apart, because a hard-zero silhouette
  and a quantised noise floor have different spectra.
- **`BinaryThreshold` replaced by `image > 0.5` in `dilate_mask_mm`.** The first implementation
  used `sitk.BinaryThreshold(mask, 0.5, 1e9, 1, 0)`; an upper bound that is not representable in
  the input pixel type silently **inverts** a uint8 mask. MEASURED: the "dilated brain mask"
  contained 7 396 714 of 8 530 021 voxels (87 %, i.e. the complement of the brain plus its
  dilation) instead of 2 849 295 (33 %). It also made each registration 2.4x slower (154 s
  against 64 s per IXI subject) because the metric sampled the whole head and neck. Caught by
  sanity-checking the voxel count against the 1 886 574-voxel brain mask before trusting it.
- **The registration transform is optimised in place as an `Euler3DTransform`**
  (`SetInitialTransform(optimised, inPlace=True)`); `inPlace=False` rejected because
  `Execute` then returns a `CompositeTransform`, which has no `GetMatrix`/`GetCenter` and can
  neither be written to the sidecar as six interpretable parameters nor composed in the
  accuracy test.
- **The SimpleITK grid and the nibabel affine are asserted to agree**
  (`mri.assert_grid_matches_affine`) rather than assumed. The whole orientation contract rests

## 6. Open questions, risks, follow-ups

- **`sitk.BinaryThreshold` with an out-of-range upper bound silently inverts a mask** —
  severity: high for any later mask code — owner: T4.x and anyone writing mask logic. On a
  `uint8` image, `sitk.BinaryThreshold(mask, 0.5, 1e9, 1, 0)` returned the **complement**
  (6 643 447 voxels instead of 1 886 574), because `1e9` is not representable in the pixel
  type. It cost this ticket a 2.4x slowdown and an 87 %-of-the-head metric region before it was
  caught by checking the voxel count. Use `image > 0.5`, or an upper bound of `1`, and always
  assert the resulting voxel count against the input's.
- **The Mattes mutual information is not a pure alignment score across subjects** — severity:
  medium — owner: whoever reads `registration_report.md`. Its value also depends on each
  acquisition's contrast, SNR and field of view, so the `median + 3 MAD` gate is a detector of
  gross failures, not a ranking of alignment quality. The visual gate
  (`qc/registration_sheet.png`) carries the rest, and `--exclude` records anything flagged
  there. A metric-independent check (for instance Dice of a threshold-based head mask against
  the template's, or the residual translation and rotation magnitudes, which are already in
  every sidecar) would be a stronger gate if a later ticket needs one.
- **The metric is flat near the optimum on real heads, to roughly a degree** — severity: medium
  — owner: T1.3. MEASURED: on IXI002 the converged metric is -0.15442 under two stopping rules
  whose poses differ by 0.96 degrees and 0.24 mm; across relaxation factors the same subject's
  poses spread by up to 4.96 degrees at an almost identical metric. At a 70 mm radius one
  degree is about 1.2 mm, roughly one voxel, so a residual pose spread of that order survives
  into the cohort. This is the floor that rigid MI registration can reach on these data, and it
  is the right number to quote when `learning/03` §20's caveat ("a registered raw cohort sits
  somewhere between the two columns, and nobody has measured where") is finally answered: the
  coarse-scale variance that T1.3 measures still contains a sub-voxel pose component.
- **`counts.padding_fraction` is not the acquisition's padding** — severity: low but easy to
  misread — owner: T1.3 and the report. `03-data-format.md` §5 defines it as the mean fraction
  of exactly-zero pixels, and on these cohorts that is dominated by the background noise
  rounding to zero under the uint8 quantisation, not by the field of view. Use
  `counts.fov_padding_fraction` for the geometric quantity that `learning/03` §19 discusses.
- **The OASIS-1 axis map now has two independent confirmations**, the original FOMO60K
  cross-correlation and this ticket's 12-of-12 atlas mutual-information margin, so it is no
  longer a single point of failure. It remains an empirical claim about a header-less format:
  `tests/preprocess/test_mri_raw.py::test_oasis_axis_map_matches_the_ported_reference` fails if
  anyone changes it.
