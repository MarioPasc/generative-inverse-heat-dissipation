**Template geometry, measured (not assumed)** — `nibabel` on
`tpl-MNI152NLin2009cAsym_res-01_T1w.nii.gz`: shape `(193, 229, 193)`, zooms `(1, 1, 1)`,
affine `diag(1, 1, 1)` with offset `(-96, -132, -78)`, axcodes `('R', 'A', 'S')`. The brain mask
shares the affine and is binary `{0, 1}`. SimpleITK reads the same file with
`direction = diag(-1, -1, 1)` (the RAS→LPS flip) and `origin = (96, 132, -78)`, so **the SimpleITK
voxel index order equals the nibabel one** and no permutation is needed anywhere between the two
libraries. Therefore: MNI origin voxel `(i0, j0) = (96, 132)`; the 192 window is `i ∈ [0, 192)`,
`j ∈ [36, 228)`; `z_mm(k) = k - 78`, i.e. the ten planes are
`[-23, -15, -7, 1, 9, 18, 26, 34, 42, 50]` mm. All of this is *derived from the affine and
asserted* in `mri.template_geometry`, never hard-coded.

**Decisions taken during the work** (each with the alternative rejected and why):

- **Optimiser: `SetOptimizerAsRegularStepGradientDescent(learningRate=1.0, minStep=1e-4,
  numberOfIterations=300, relaxationFactor=0.5, gradientMagnitudeTolerance=1e-8)`** — LBFGSB
  rejected. Mattes MI with random 20 % sampling is a *stochastic* objective; LBFGSB builds a
  quasi-Newton Hessian estimate from finite differences of successive gradients and its line search
  assumes a smooth deterministic function, so sampling noise degrades the curvature estimate and it
  is not the recipe SimpleITK's own rigid-registration examples use. Regular-step gradient descent
  only needs the sign of the directional change (it halves the step when the gradient reverses),
  which is robust to that noise, and combined with `SetOptimizerScalesFromPhysicalShift` the step
  is in millimetres of physical displacement, so `learningRate = 1.0` means "1 mm of shift" and
  `minStep = 1e-4` mm is three orders of magnitude below the 0.5 mm accuracy target of the tests.
  The metric sampling seed is fixed (2026) so the objective is deterministic within a run and the
  whole pipeline is reproducible.
- **10 mm dilation of the brain mask by exact Euclidean distance map**
  (`SignedMaurerDistanceMap(..., useImageSpacing=True) <= 10 mm`) rather than
  `BinaryDilate` with a ball structuring element of radius 10 voxels — the distance-map threshold
  is the exact 10 mm morphological dilation for any spacing (it does not depend on the template
  being 1 mm isotropic), and it is O(N) instead of O(N·r³). `BinaryDilate` rejected because a
  discrete ball of radius 10 is both slower and only an approximation of a 10 mm ball.
- **OASIS-1 axis map ported verbatim** from `analysis/unprocessed_loaders.py::load_volume_ras`:
  `volume = np.transpose(np.squeeze(raw), (2, 0, 1))[::-1]` with
  `zooms = (zooms_raw[2], zooms_raw[0], zooms_raw[1])`, giving index order `(i→R, j→A, k→S)` and
  spacing `(1.25, 1.0, 1.0)` mm. nibabel's SPM2-Analyze affine (`diag(-1, 1, 1.25)`) is
  **ignored**: Analyze 7.5 carries no usable orientation and that affine is nibabel's guess. The
  ported map was established empirically (mid-plane contact sheets plus translation-aligned
  cross-correlation against the FOMO60K copy of the same scan, 12/12 subjects, mean normalised
  cross-correlation margin +0.048). This ticket re-verifies the left–right polarity independently:
  see the "OASIS L/R polarity" check in §4.
- **IXI loaded straight through `sitk.ReadImage`** — no nibabel `as_closest_canonical`
  reorientation. The IXI NIfTI affine is valid and slightly oblique (measured:
  `axcodes ('P', 'S', 'R')`, direction cosines off-axis by ~0.12); SimpleITK keeps the exact
  direction cosines in physical space, so the registration works in true physical coordinates and
  the single resampling absorbs the obliquity. `as_closest_canonical` rejected because it *rounds*
  the direction to the nearest axis-aligned one, which is an uncorrected rotation of up to ~7° for
  these volumes and would be baked into the data.
- **The registered volume is cached, not the slices.** Sidecar JSON carries the metric, the
  iteration count, the stop condition and the transform parameters, so the quality gate can be
  recomputed without re-registering and a `--limit` dry run warms the cache for the full run.
- **MAD in the failure gate is the raw median absolute deviation** (`median(|x − median|)`), not the
  consistency-scaled `1.4826 · MAD`. The ticket writes "median + 3 × MAD" without a constant, and
  the raw form is the stricter of the two (for a Gaussian it cuts at ≈2.02 σ, ≈2.2 % of subjects,
  which is what "< 5 % expected" in the acceptance list describes; the scaled form would cut at
  3 σ ≈ 0.13 % and would effectively disable the gate). Both thresholds and both failure counts are
  reported in `registration_report.md` so the choice is auditable.
