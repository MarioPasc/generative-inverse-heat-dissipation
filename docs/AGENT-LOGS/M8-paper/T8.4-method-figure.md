# Task: T8.4 — FM, the method figure of the paper (wave W18)

## Mission

Build the figure that explains Inverse Heat Dissipation Models (IHDM) in the frequency language of
the paper. It has three parts:

1. **A rounded box at the top:** an MRI slice written as a weighted sum of DCT modes, with the
   modes as images, the weights as $w_{i,j}$, and the modes of one octave sharing a colour, plus
   an index-plane inset where each octave is a coloured quarter-annulus.
2. **A forward row and a reverse row of heat-diffusion states**, one macro-step per octave.
   - Forward arrows carry "−(modes of that octave)" above and $q(\mathbf u_k\mid\mathbf u_0)$

3. **A thin vertical panel on the right**, spanning the full height:
   - IXI's between-image variance per octave;
   - the number of reverse levels each schedule gives each octave (default against matched);
   - two lines saying what the schedule does.

- `ihdm/analysis/paper_fm.py` and the CLI `ihdm/cli/paper_fm.py`;

## Why this exists

This is a UAM course project; the paper is due 2026-11-03.

- **The question.** Are IHDM's natural-image defaults (prior W/2 = 96 px; log-spaced levels, i.e.
  equal levels per octave) right for brain MRI, or does MRI benefit from matching them to its
  spectrum and to its shared anatomy?
- **What the reader must understand from this figure:**
  1. an image is a sum of DCT modes, grouped into octaves;
  2. the forward heat process removes octaves from fine to coarse, and generation restores them
     from coarse to fine;
  3. the prior decides where generation starts (W/2: almost nothing kept; W/8: the octaves below
     about 1.8 c/img kept);
  4. the schedule decides how many of the 200 levels each octave gets, which matters because MRI's
     variance is not equal across octaves.

**Decisions taken during the work** (each with the alternative rejected and why):

- **Octaves in the rows: 6 explicit macro-steps plus an ellipsis folding the two finest
  (64–96 and 32–64 c/img)**. Rejected: all 8. Nine state thumbnails of at least 0.40 in, with
  eight arrows, do not fit in the 4.3 in left of the panel when each arrow carries its own
  $p_\theta(\mathbf u_{k-1}\mid\mathbf u_k)$ label (0.62 in at 7 pt in DejaVu mathtext, 0.50 in in
  stixsans). The two folded octaves are also invisible at print size: a 0.42 in thumbnail is
  126 px at 300 dpi, Nyquist 63 c/img. The ellipsis says what it folds: "−(32–96 c/img)" forward,
  "+(32–96 c/img)" reverse (main's condition 3a). The panel and the caption still give all 8
  octaves' counts (condition 3b).
- **The rows start with the symbol "x ⋯", not an x thumbnail.** Rejected: an x thumbnail. It
  costs about 0.5 in and would push the pitch below the $p_\theta$ label width. The slice is shown
  large in the top box directly above, and u(1.35 px) is indistinguishable from it at print size.
- **Reverse row: a right-to-left mirror under the forward row.** Rejected: a second left-to-right
  row starting at the prior. In the mirror each column is one state and one octave, so "−(octave)"
  and "+(octave)" share a column and the σ_B label between the rows serves both. The arrowheads
  carry the direction. A left-to-right row would put the same octave at mirrored positions in the
  two rows.
- **No model sample at the end of the reverse row.** Rejected: the `ixi_A3_s1` held-out sample.
  It would end the *ideal* path with a *model* state, which the reader cannot tell apart, and
  there is no width for a 0.42 in thumbnail. `--eval-dir` and `--work` are accepted (frozen
  interface) and not read; `FM.md` says so.
- **Vertical panel: fine (64–96) at the top, coarse (0.5–1) at the bottom.** This is the order in
  which the forward row removes the octaves (its left-to-right reads as the panel's
  top-to-bottom), and frequency increases upward as on a usual axis. Rejected: coarse at the top,
  the reading order of the top equation. The prompt preferred alignment with the forward row.
- **Mathtext font: `stixsans`, in a local `rc_context` (`fm_style`) inside `paper_fm`; not in
  `paper_style`.** Rejected: the default DejaVu mathtext, whose $p_\theta$ label (0.62 in) is wider
  than the 0.56 in pitch. This is a known cross-figure difference: F1 and F2 use DejaVu mathtext
  (main's condition 3c).
- **Octave colours: one violet-purple hue (OKLCH H = 305°, L 0.28 → 0.78, C ≤ 0.17), dark =
  coarse, light = fine; DC `#9a9893` grey.** It passes the dataviz skill's ordinal check
  (`validate_palette.js --ordinal`): monotone lightness, adjacent ΔL ≥ 0.06, single hue,
  lightest step 2.05:1 on white. The hue sits 50° from the default blue (256°) and 52° from the
  A2′ pink (357°). Rejected:
  - viridis and cividis, which contain blue, green and yellow close to the arm colours;
  - the palette's violet slot (H = 284°), whose light steps drift to periwinkle, near the
    default blue.

  The lightest step (64–96) is never a chip, because that octave is folded. It is visible as the
  outer quarter-annulus of the inset and as the 64–96 bar on the panel's light fill (main's
  condition 4; checked in the PNG).
- **Index-plane inset with a log radius (r = 1 + log₂ n, DC disc r < 1).** Rejected: a linear
  radius. Octaves 0.5–4 c/img occupy n < 8, i.e. 4% of the axis, which is invisible in a 0.8 in
  inset. The quarter-annuli are exact in this mapping, and the caption says "log radius". Also
  drawn:
  - the corner modes above 96 c/img, light grey (the image of the square beyond the last ring);
  - the 10 shown modes, as dots.
- **Mode thumbnails on one grey scale, ±2/W.** The diagonal modes reach |φ| = 2/W, so amplitudes
  are comparable and the DC mode reads as uniform light grey. Rejected: a per-mode scale, under
  which the DC mode renders blank white.
- **Representative modes: (0, 2c₀) at the octave's lower edge and (2c₀, 2c₀) at its log centre
  (c = √2 c₀).** Asserted to lie in the octave.
- **Two-line equation in the top box, weights $w_{i,j}$ under each mode, octave bar and label
